# EVRP features for Baldes: literature and implementation roadmap

Research date: **2026-10-20**. Repository inspected on local `main`, including the recently merged multi-network enumeration/MIP work (HEAD observed as `91ce712` during this review). **Local planning document; deliberately untracked.** Nothing below is a claim that EVRP support has already been implemented or benchmarked.

## Recommended direction

Build an **electric vehicle routing problem with time windows (EVRPTW)** extension around Baldes's route-based master. Start with deterministic travel/energy, customer capacities, repeatable charging-station visits, and linear partial charging; then support station-specific piecewise-linear charging curves. Reuse the master, fleet networks, stabilization, cuts and MIP backend, while introducing a charging-aware route evaluator and pricing implementation.

The first deliverable should be an EV model and an independent schedule validator, followed by a fixed-customer-order charging evaluator. These give us a correctness oracle before changing labeling, dominance or bidirectional joins. A full-recharge mode is a useful intermediate baseline, but the target should allow multiple partial recharges.

For exact nonlinear pricing, read **Nafstad, Desaulniers and Stålhane (2025)** first. For an alternative pricing decomposition, evaluate **Jacquillat and Lo, revision September 2026**, after the baseline is measured. For the heuristic side, prioritize charging-aware decoding and route repair over changing the population search framework. These priorities are engineering judgments based on the sources and repository inspection below.

## Scope and evidence

This is a targeted literature search, emphasizing 2024–2026 papers and older work needed to implement their methods. Searches covered exact EVRPTW algorithms, nonlinear/partial charging, subpath column generation, heterogeneous fleets/stations, fixed-route charging and recent heuristics. Evidence comes from publisher pages, accessible paper texts, author/institutional manuscripts, author repositories and benchmark organizers. Search snippets were used for discovery; the links below identify the primary evidence used for technical conclusions.

There is **no single state-of-the-art leaderboard for EVRP**. Full versus partial charging, customer time windows, load capacity, heterogeneous fleets, station capacity, electricity prices and objective definitions change the problem. Reported customer counts and speedups below belong to each paper's own experiments. No Baldes EVRP experiments were run for this document, and no cross-paper timing normalization was performed.

Status labels distinguish a journal publication from an arXiv manuscript. A DOI containing `2024` or `2025` is not necessarily the journal issue year. The current version of arXiv:2407.02640 also has a different title and scope from its initial 2024 version.

## Proposed model and architecture

This section is a proposed design, not an assertion that any one paper supplies a ready-made Baldes implementation.

### Initial supported problem

- One depot initially; directed travel-time, distance/cost and nonnegative energy matrices stored separately.
- Delivery-only demand, load capacity, customer service times, hard service-start time windows and a depot return horizon.
- Deterministic energy independent of carried load in the first version; no regeneration initially.
- Full initial battery, configurable terminal reserve, explicit charging availability and connector compatibility.
- Multiple station visits per route, including revisiting a physical station; customers served once in a feasible integer solution.
- Partial linear charging as the first target, with full-recharge-only as an explicitly named baseline. Then station/type-specific monotone piecewise-linear curves.
- Unlimited simultaneous charger availability initially. Station capacity, queuing, tariffs, battery degradation and traffic dependence are separate later variants.
- Explicit objective mode: distance, or fleet-size-then-distance where required by the selected benchmark. Do not invent an arbitrary large fixed vehicle penalty as a substitute for a documented lexicographic objective.

For a station curve `H_s(t)` giving energy reached from empty, charging from `b_in` to `b_out` takes `H_s^-1(b_out) - H_s^-1(b_in)`, with `0 <= b_in <= b_out <= B`. A linear rate `r_s` gives `(b_out - b_in)/r_s`. The curve/inverse relation is used in E1's charging model. An implementation must validate breakpoint order, units, domain, continuity and invertibility rather than assume these properties from raw input.

### Modules and responsibilities

| Proposed component | Responsibility and integration point |
|---|---|
| `evrp::Problem` and parser | Physical locations, customer/station roles, vehicle types, units, energy data, charging functions and objective policy. Translate public instance formats without silently changing rounding or charging policy. New module name is a proposal. |
| `evrp::ChargingCurve` | Linear/PWL evaluation, inverse and charging duration. Store canonical breakpoints and test all boundary cases. |
| `evrp::Schedule` and validator | Ordered travel/service/charging actions with arrival/departure times, SoC before/after charging, technology and original physical IDs. Replay original data independently of pricing caches and buckets. |
| Fixed-order charging evaluator | For a customer order, find station insertions and charge amounts, or report infeasible/unknown. A time/work limit is `Unknown`, not a proof that no charging schedule exists. Reuse for HGS repair, seeding and tests. |
| EV pricing oracle | Return reduced-cost routes, their schedules, and a clearly identified pricing completion/bound status to the master. Keep coupled charging state out of the scalar kernel's independent-coordinate assumptions. |
| Fleet/master adapter | Shared customer rows and one network per compatible vehicle/depot type; fixed/type costs and fleet bounds. Existing network plumbing is reusable, but an EV oracle interface still needs implementation. |
| EV column and snapshot metadata | Preserve charging semantics and schedules across deduplication, branching, fixed routes, node forks and MIP copies. Invalidate caches after dual/cut/branch changes where their values depend on those changes. |

The master should continue to select complete routes. E2's customer subpaths are **internal pricing objects**, not automatically new master columns. This distinction allows direct and subpath pricing to be compared without changing the master relaxation at the same time.

For nonlinear pricing, evaluate a PWL departure-time-versus-SoC function per label, following E1, rather than keeping just the single earliest time and a single battery value. Include ordinary reduced cost, load, customer memory and cut/branch state. Forward extension, backward extension, pointwise dominance, joining and schedule reconstruction need compatible definitions. Start with forward pricing; introduce bidirectionality only after tiny exhaustive comparisons pass.

For schedule-independent distance cost and uncapacitated stations, an entire feasible charging family may correspond to the same route column. When costs, branching or occupancy depend on the schedule, this compression needs another argument. Never merge two schedules merely because their travel arcs match if they have different relevant master coefficients or objective values.

### Correctness requirements before optimization

1. **Energy is a controlled state.** Fixed negative consumption on an arc cannot express arbitrary partial recharge with the matching elapsed time. Disposable waiting on a battery coordinate would permit energy creation at ordinary vertices unless the semantics are deliberately changed.
2. **Customer elementarity is different from physical vertex simplicity.** Repeated station visits can be legitimate. Any station-copy limit or hop limit needs a proved bound for the selected model, or an explicit restricted-model/heuristic label.
3. **Cycles need a termination argument.** Handle zero-duration/zero-energy station transitions and repeated charging actions explicitly. Do not rely on the existing physical-simple-path bound to establish finiteness of EV pricing or enumeration.
4. **Dominance must preserve feasible completions.** With partial charging, scalar earliest-time comparisons can discard the useful side of a time/SoC trade-off. Validate every proposed dominance reduction against exhaustive tiny instances with it disabled.
5. **Heuristic sparsification is separate from exact pricing.** A nearest-station shortlist can find columns quickly but cannot prove that no improving route exists. A complete pass must retain nondominated alternatives, including faster but more distant chargers.
6. **Charging schedules are part of incumbent evidence.** Check travel energy, charge gains, charging duration, service windows, horizon, load, compatibility, coverage, fleet limits and objective using original data. Replacing the scalar replay function is not sufficient unless every consumer uses the EV contract.
7. **Certificates need new eligibility rules.** Existing directed scalar sums do not establish nonlinear-function feasibility or an EV pricing lower bound. Start with explicitly numerical EV results and independently validated feasible schedules; enable certified bounds only after suitable conservative function arithmetic/relaxations are implemented.
8. **Finite charger capacity is fleet-wide.** An aggregate limit on the total number of station visits cannot enforce concurrent occupancy. Use explicit intervals and a scheduling model/decomposition when this variant is introduced, as motivated by F3.

Before external benchmarks, build 3–8-customer reference cases with: no reachable station; a route feasible only with partial recharge; a required station revisit; two charging stops between consecutive customers; a farther fast charger beating a near slow charger; arrival exactly at a PWL breakpoint; early arrival and waiting; charger/vehicle incompatibility; fractional shared-master solutions; fixed routes; branching that changes a previously best station path; and zero-cost/time cycle edge cases.

For each comparable benchmark run, store model/version/hash, charging policy and curves, objective mode, rounding and units, CPU/threads, compiler/build flags, seeds, time limits, first feasible time, final validated objective, root LP bound, final bound/gap with proof mode, CG/pricing time, labels generated/retained, dominance/join counts, charging-function segment counts, peak memory, MIP nodes, validation failures and terminal status. Report median/dispersion across heuristic seeds. Do not convert `Unknown`, incomplete pricing or a restricted-pool optimum into a global optimality claim.

Useful ablations are: scalar/full-recharge baseline versus partial-linear pricing; partial-linear versus PWL under the same data; forward versus bidirectional; direct versus subpath pricing; dense complete pricing versus heuristic sparsification plus complete fallback; station repair versus joint split/charge decoding; and enumeration/MIP disabled versus enabled under an EV-specific completeness contract. ## Open design decisions and limits of this review

- Choose the first public benchmark's exact objective and charging policy before writing the parser. Do not let the existing VRPTW input conventions decide EV units implicitly.
- Decide whether the first exact route representation includes charging-station sequences or compresses them into charging alternatives on a customer multigraph. Either needs explicit reconstruction and a completeness argument.
- Decide how a master pricing-oracle interface can accept EV-specific label state without forcing the scalar engine to handle arbitrary callbacks on its hot path. This review identifies the seam; it does not prescribe a final Rust trait design.
- A fixed charging schedule can be validated independently without proving that it is optimal for its route. The latter is needed where its cost is used as the route's optimal objective contribution or in an exact pricing argument.
- The 2026 subpath revision and decoder preprint are recent candidates, not independently reproduced results. The primary nonlinear bidirectional BPC reference is published in 2025; retain both approaches for a measured comparison.
- Full text was accessible for E1, E2, E4, E5, H1, H2, H3, the H5 manuscript and F3. E3's author manuscript is older than its journal version. H4/H6/H7 and F1/F2 were assessed primarily from publisher/author records and available descriptions; detailed equation-level ports require reading their complete versions. No referenced implementation was installed or executed.
- Learning-based and stochastic EVRP methods were outside the first implementation shortlist. They can be evaluated later as column/neighbor proposals against this model and validator; they do not remove the need for complete pricing when a bound is claimed.

The immediate next action is to implement **the EV problem schema, charging curves and schedule replay**, with explicitly supported assumptions and tiny fixtures. That foundation supports both the heuristic path and the exact pricing path without claiming capabilities from the existing `Evrp` enum name.
