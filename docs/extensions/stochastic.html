Stochastic demands and replenishment recourse

baldes::extensions::stochastic implements a CVRPSD extension based on Tomazella, Munari and Morabito (2026), Pseudo-Compact Formulations and Branch-and-Cut Approaches for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem with Stochastic Demands: https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Tomazella_Munari_Morabito_2026_Formulations-1.pdf

The model assumes independent finite discrete customer demands, integer vehicle capacity, and demand realizations no larger than capacity. The depot is vertex 0, and demand entry i belongs to customer i + 1. Costs may be asymmetric; callers should supply a matrix satisfying the triangle inequality. Construction checks matrix dimensions, nonnegative finite costs and zero diagonal, but does not check every triangle. A priori routes visit each assigned customer once, and their total expected demand cannot exceed capacity.

Supported policies

  • Policy::Classical implements P1, equation (9): replenish at the customer on failure. If a customer empties the vehicle exactly, replenish before visiting the next customer. No replenishment is charged after the last customer.
  • Policy::OptimalRestocking implements P3, equation (11): at each remaining-load state choose whether to replenish before continuing or risk a customer failure.

Problem::evaluate_route returns planned travel cost, expected recourse cost, their sum, and expected demand. It uses a backward dynamic program with Q + 1 states and two vectors, taking O(route length * (Q+1) * support size) time. Probability sums within 1e-12 of one are normalized during Demand::new. Computation uses f64; no Monte Carlo sampling is involved.

BALDES pricing and branch-and-price

Problem::pricing_graph compiles to the ordinary BALDES physical graph, using CoupledPlugin objective costs for expected recourse. Problem::price runs pricing::Solver::price_phase_partial, including bucket labeling and optional bidirectional joins. This prices the stochastic objective directly. The compiled graph also works with RoutingContext::set_partitioning and the shared routing root master in numerical bound mode; regression tests cover its phase-I cost suppression and restoration.

The default search traverses the physical graph in reverse and carries a Q + 1 Bellman continuation vector. Each extension prepends one Bellman operator in O((Q+1) * support size), avoiding repeated prefix evaluation. For visited-customer subsets, componentwise continuation-vector dominance is safe because Bellman expectation and minimum operators are monotone. Expected capacity is summed in customer-ID order, independently of route order.

PricingOptions::bidirectional = true retains the original bidirectional adapter: forward states retain their complete customer sequence and joins replay suffixes. Problem::pricing_graph also retains that original physical orientation for shared-master integration. Generic completion bounds and reversal reuse remain disabled for cost plugins. Other cost plugins retain equality dominance unless they explicitly supply a cost-preserving dominance contract. Large instances can still exhaust their label or time budget.

The adapter supplies full elementarity through NG memory and supports at most NG_MEMORY_CAPACITY customers: 15 by default, or 31/63 with ng-memory-31 / ng-memory-63. The route domain has at most n+1 arcs, including departure and return. A loose scalar mean-load window is only a necessary filter; canonical forward resource replay enforces the expected-demand capacity condition. Cost magnitudes are checked against BALDES's route-pool sentinel; unusually large units must be rescaled.

Column generation starts with scalar NG-route prepricing when the customer count exceeds ColumnGenerationOptions::ng_size (default 8; zero disables it). For configured sizes above four, it first tries a four-customer neighborhood. If that stage neither finds a new improving route nor proves convergence, it tries the configured size, then falls back to stochastic Bellman pricing. Completed lower bounds are retained across stages; interrupted stages cannot certify convergence by themselves. Problem::price_ng builds nearest-customer memories in the ordinary BALDES engine. Its first sweep retains 16 labels per bucket to find columns quickly; these heuristic sweeps supply no lower bound. A complete scalar sweep can supply a bound. The master allocates at most 100,000 labels and two seconds per NG neighborhood, subject to the remaining pricing and solve time budgets. During the expected-cost phase, every eighth master iteration also starts each NG neighborhood with a complete scalar sweep, bypassing the bounded heuristic sweeps. This gives bound-producing pricing a chance even when earlier iterations keep finding improving columns. The same label and time limits apply; an interrupted sweep cannot certify convergence.

The scalar objective charges travel plus each visited customer's minimum nonpositive preventive detour. Bellman recourse is bounded below by the sum of these customer charges, including on nonmetric matrices. Hence the minimum of a completely priced NG relaxation is a global lower bound on true elementary stochastic reduced costs. It proves convergence only if it excludes improving columns within tolerance. It does not generally equal true stochastic pricing.

Cyclic walks never enter the master or an incumbent. Candidate elementary routes are evaluated with the full P1/P3 Bellman program, including all branch duals. When cycles obscure useful candidates, decremental state-space refinement adds the repeated customer to memories along its cycle and reprices; every elementary route remains in the relaxation. Label/time exhaustion retains earlier valid bounds but cannot turn an observed minimum into a bound. Primal pool/complement search runs during column generation, and feasible integer restricted-master snapshots can become incumbents even before pricing converges.

Use stochastic_instance ... --ng-size 8 for this pipeline and --ng-size 0 for the Bellman-only adapter. Problem::price itself retains its original exact stochastic pricing contract. The benchmark runner also accepts --ng-size.

Master<M: LpMaster> builds exact customer-cover rows and an upper fleet row. Phase I uses artificial variables and zero physical/recourse costs; phase II restores total expected costs. Every seed/generated route is independently re-evaluated. Complete pricing is required before reporting LP convergence or phase-I infeasibility. Restricted-pool objectives are never reported as global bounds.

Problem::solve_with accepts a factory for fresh LP backends. Its arc branch-and-price driver branches on fractional directed arc flows: zero removes the arc from subsequent pricing; one adds a required-arc equality row whose dual enters pricing. Each child performs two-phase column generation. Solving only an integer restricted route pool is not used to establish optimality. Problem::solve is the Clp convenience wrapper, available with features=clp:

Rust
use baldes::extensions::stochastic::{Policy, SolveOptions};
let answer = problem.solve(Policy::OptimalRestocking, 3, SolveOptions::default())?;

An optional HGS warm start searches a deterministic CVRP with the same cost matrix and mean customer demands. HGS uses integer loads, so means are scaled and rounded down for the heuristic. Every candidate route is independently checked for elementarity and the original expected-capacity constraint, then evaluated with the selected stochastic recourse policy before entering the column pool. A complete HGS plan becomes an incumbent only if it covers each customer once, respects the fleet limit, and all its routes pass validation. HGS candidates do not establish bounds or optimality. Its time budget is part of the overall solve time; initialization can overrun the requested limit.

Set SolveOptions::hgs_warm_start to Some(HgsWarmStart { time_limit, seed }). The result's hgs_warm_start summary reports accepted and rejected candidate counts and whether the HGS plan passed validation. For generated instances:

Terminal
cargo run --release --features clp,ng-memory-63 --example stochastic_instance -- \
  instances/stochastic/generated_salavati_style/synthetic-n20-m2-r01-fr0.90.json \
  --solve --seconds 30 --hgs-seconds 3 --hgs-seed 0 --json

Run the complete example:

Terminal
cargo run -p baldes --features clp --example stochastic

SolveStatus::Optimal means the branch-and-price tree is exhausted under the configured floating-point tolerances. It is not an outward-rounded arithmetic certificate. Node, iteration, pricing-work, and time limits retain an incomplete status. Completed routes and labeling statistics survive pricing interruptions; partial routes can improve the restricted master but never establish convergence. Best-fit starts with several nearest-neighbor/shuffled orders, travel 2-opt, and a bounded exact-cover/complement search over known columns provide feasible incumbents without waiting for complete pricing. Heuristic packing failure does not prove infeasibility.

Stopped solves return the best incumbent found and a numerical lower bound. The initial degree relaxation accounts for negative recourse on nonmetric matrices. A hop-bounded shortest-walk relaxation supplies a global reduced-cost lower bound even after incomplete pricing; shifting the fleet dual by its negative part gives a valid full-master bound. Open nodes retain the best such bound or their inherited bound. PricingResult::minimum_reduced_cost is absent after incomplete pricing; lower_bound is the separate relaxation bound. Generic directed plugin certification returns unknown for cost-bearing plugins, preventing a feasibility-only proof from silently assigning zero recourse cost.

Exact tiny-instance baseline

Problem::solve_tiny enumerates elementary route orders, retains the cheapest order for each customer subset, and solves the resulting set-partitioning problem by subset dynamic programming. max_vehicles is an upper limit, not an equality. All customers, including zero-demand customers, must be assigned.

This deliberately supports at most nine customers. route_budget bounds the number of evaluated routes; exceeding it returns an error, not a feasible answer or an optimality certificate. Ok(None) means that exhaustive search proved fleet infeasibility. An empty customer set has a zero-cost solution. Optimality is with respect to the supplied distributions and ordinary floating-point comparisons.

Remaining boundaries

The paper's arc-based MIP, rule-based and penalty recourse policies, Python bindings, stronger stochastic dominance, specialized fast joins, and stochastic cut separation are not implemented. The branch-and-price driver currently adds arc branch rows and uses no additional cutting planes.

Verification:

Terminal
cargo test -p baldes --features clp --test stochastic --test stochastic_pricing

Tests cover hand-calculated costs, comparison against a separate scenario tree, BALDES pricing against exhaustive route orders in both directions, phase-I cost suppression, forbidden/required arc handling, full-route LP comparisons, integer optima against the tiny oracle, a fractional root requiring branching, and incomplete-work statuses.
