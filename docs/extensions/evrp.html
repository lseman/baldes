EVRP implementation status

Documentation index

baldes::extensions::evrp is the initial model and independent schedule replay layer. It supports deterministic directed travel with separate time, energy and cost, delivery capacity, customer service-start windows, repeat charging-station visits, and explicit partial charging with station-specific piecewise-linear curves. Fixed-sequence charging optimization and reference bidirectional direct pricing and a two-phase subpath prototype are available. Both feed the same route-based LP master and column-generation adapter.

Charging curves map energy to cumulative time from empty. Charging from a to b takes T(b) - T(a); charge_for applies the inverse curve and caps at full charge. Knots must start at zero and increase strictly in both coordinates. Non-concave charging profiles are accepted. Energy and time units are supplied consistently by callers.

Problem::replay takes an explicit closed route with an amount charged per visit and an initial departure time. The vehicle starts full. It reports every arrival, service start, departure and arrival/departure energy, plus total travel cost and delivered load. Charging follows station service and must finish by closing; customers only require service start within their window. Waiting is allowed and consumes no energy. A route can cover a customer subset, but cannot repeat customers or contain intermediate depots. Comparisons use strict floating-point bounds with no implicit tolerance. Travel costs must be nonnegative; pricing reduced costs are not inputs to this validator.

Replay checks feasibility of a supplied charging schedule, not optimality of its charging decisions. Tariffs, charger congestion, regeneration, mixed vehicle compatibility and station insertion are not implemented.

Fixed stop sequence optimizer

Problem::optimize_fixed_order(order, departure, ChargingOptions::default()) chooses continuous partial charging amounts on a supplied sequence of customers and stations. It minimizes completion time. Travel cost is fixed by that sequence, and charging has no separate monetary cost in the current model. Station visits may repeat; customer visits must be elementary.

The algorithm propagates piecewise-affine earliest departure time as a function of exact remaining energy. At a station, for cumulative charging time T and incoming time function F, it evaluates T(x) + min { F(q) - T(q) : q <= x }. On each affine interval the minimum is attained at an endpoint or at q = x. All these candidates are generated; their lower envelope is split at domain endpoints and function intersections. The construction permits non-concave charging curves without an energy grid. Time-window waiting is another piecewise-affine operation. Every retained piece keeps its predecessor and energy mapping for schedule reconstruction.

The result distinguishes Optimal, Infeasible, WorkLimit, and NumericalFailure. Optimal is the optimizer's result in floating-point arithmetic, not an interval-arithmetic certificate. Every returned schedule passes independent strict replay. Reconstruction that fails replay reports NumericalFailure, never Infeasible. The work budget counts generated pieces and envelope comparisons; reaching it yields no feasibility or optimality claim. The envelope implementation prioritizes a transparent reference algorithm over large-instance speed.

Tests compare charging allocations against independent quarter-energy grid enumeration on tiny heterogeneous/non-concave instances whose optimum lies on that grid, and check waiting, repeated stations, exact windows and work limits. These tests do not establish performance on published benchmarks.

Direct bidirectional pricing

Problem::price_direct(duals, fleet_dual, departure, DirectOptions::default()) prices elementary customer routes with repeatable station visits. The dual vector has one entry per location and must be zero at stations and depots. Reduced cost is travel cost minus customer duals minus one fleet dual. The best replay-validated route is returned even when its reduced cost is nonnegative. No subset-row cuts, ng relaxation or branching restrictions are accepted yet.

The implementation uses forward earliest-departure functions F(e) and backward latest-departure functions B(e) at exact departure energy e. Backward functions exclude processing at the meeting node, already processed by the forward label. Internally backward times are negated to share lower-envelope code. Backward extension undoes charging, station closing, service, customer windows and travel in the appropriate order. At a station the core operation is max { B(y) - T(y) : y >= q } + T(q) - service, with station closing applied to departure before charging is undone.

Dominance conservatively requires equal visited-customer sets, no greater reduced cost, no more used arcs and pointwise time dominance over the entire other function's domain. The function comparison covers domain gaps and interior crossings; endpoint-only scalar tests are insufficient. Joins require compatible customer sets, capacity, total arc count and some common energy with F(e) <= B(e). Both directions retain energy mappings for reconstruction; every returned complete route passes strict schedule replay.

This is an E1-inspired reference implementation of the coherent function extension/dominance/join approach described by Nafstad, Desaulniers and Stålhane (2025), Sections 3–4. The prefix/suffix affine-envelope construction is specific to Baldes's model; the paper's faster PWL algorithm, midpoint-by-time stopping, unreachable-customer sets and acceleration techniques are not reproduced here. The current objective is travel cost, not the paper's separate route-duration extension.

Pricing statuses are explicit:

  • Bounded: all customer-serving routes with at most max_arcs travel arcs were covered. This does not certify that longer routes have no negative cost.
  • Complete: that bound covers every relevant route, using customer elementarity when there are no stations, or a conservative depot-horizon bound when all available non-self travel times are strictly positive.
  • WorkLimit / NumericalFailure: no pricing completeness claim. Any returned incumbent remains independently replay-validated.

Complete describes exhaustive model coverage in f64 arithmetic, not a rigorous real-number reduced-cost certificate. No station-repeat bound is inferred from zero-time travel. Nonnegative-cost self loops are omitted because they cannot improve a schedule or customer coverage. Work/label limits and arc limits are configurable. Both directions currently grow to half the arc bound and compare compatible label pairs; this prioritizes correctness evidence on tiny cases over large-instance speed.

Tests compare minimum reduced costs to exhaustive stop-order enumeration with the fixed-order optimizer, with dominance enabled and disabled. They also check capacity, customer elementarity, zero-time travel, work limits, bounded versus global coverage, and domain-interior dominance failures.

Two-phase subpath prototype

Problem::price_subpaths(duals, fleet_dual, departure, SubpathOptions::default()) uses the same objective, dual convention, route arc limit and completeness statuses as direct pricing. Phase one generates elementary customer orders between depot/station endpoints with no interior charging. It retains all such orders fitting a full battery and load capacity, including direct charger-to- charger moves with no customers. max_subpaths caps this phase explicitly.

Phase two composes these subpaths into complete routes. It propagates the entire continuous time/energy function through every constituent customer and endpoint, optimizes charging, and checks customer elementarity and capacity. It may prune a prefix only with equal customer history, no greater reduced cost, no more arcs and full function-domain dominance. Subpaths are never supplied as master columns. Both backends return the same complete-route representation with a replay-validated charging schedule.

The decomposition is inspired by Jacquillat and Lo, v3 (2026). The prototype deliberately retains distinct phase-one orders and carries full charging functions in phase two. It does not implement the paper's compressed resource vectors, two-sided subpath domination, adaptive ng relaxation or cuts. Thus it does not assume that scalar forward domination can replace the paper's domination requirements. Keeping the full prefix state makes composition an explicit dynamic program under this static model; the paper's speedups have not been reproduced.

Tests compare both backends with exhaustive route-order evaluation, under the same data, duals and arc bounds, with dominance enabled and disabled. Fixtures include heterogeneous/non-concave charging, repeated stations, and a route with two consecutive charging stops in both directions. Global versus bounded coverage and phase-one/phase-two work limits have separate checks.

Native physical-graph backend

Problem::price_native(duals, fleet_dual, departure, NativeOptions::default()) uses the ordinary Baldes labeling engine. Select EvPricingBackend::Native(NativeOptions::default()) in the shared EV master to use it in both feasibility and travel-cost phases. The reference direct and subpath backends remain available; the default backend is unchanged.

Problem::native_graph(...) also exposes the compiled Graph<3> for a native pricing::Solver<3>. The graph preserves location IDs and duplicates the depot at vertex locations.len() as the sink. Its active scalar resources track arc count and delivered load. Only customers receive elementarity sets, so station visits may repeat. A native PWL boolean state records whether a customer has been served; the sink requires that state to be one. Customer duals are charged on incoming arcs and the fleet dual is charged once on source departure. The delivery-only native simultaneous-load resource additionally guards load feasibility and dominance without scalar epsilon comparisons.

The native CoupledResource::Charging retains earliest departure time as a continuous function of exact remaining energy. Travel consumes energy; service starts inside its window. Stations apply the full partial-charging envelope T(x) + min_{q<=x}(F(q)-T(q)) and require service/charging to finish by closing. Heterogeneous and non-concave strictly increasing curves are supported without an energy grid. Forward labels retain owned front pieces. Backward labels retain the physical suffix; joins replay that suffix on the actual forward front. Dominance conservatively requires equal charging fronts, and reversal reuse is disabled. This resource adds no objective and cannot be a numeric parent.

After native pricing chooses a stop order, the existing fixed-order optimizer reconstructs its charging amounts and independently replays the schedule. A failed reconstruction reports numerical failure, never infeasibility or complete pricing. max_front_work bounds each individual frontier extension, including suffix replay; it is not a total pricing-work budget. max_reconstruction_work separately bounds witness reconstruction. Label limits and max_arcs bound the native search. Resource arithmetic/state failures remain unknown; the native engine may aggregate a nonzero frontier-budget exhaustion into numerical failure. The work statistic counts witness reconstruction work, not native front work.

This adapter supports at most fifteen customers by default, reflecting native exact NG local memory (31 or 63 with the ng-memory-31 or ng-memory-63 build feature), and at most 1023 input locations because it adds a sink. It uses native numerical resource/cost comparisons. Complete and Bounded retain their numerical coverage meanings; no directed-rounding certificate is provided for charging. The largest absolute reduced arc cost times max_arcs must be below 1e12 - 1, because the native engine reserves 1e12 as a sentinel; unsupported scales are rejected explicitly and require rescaling cost units. Native cuts and restrictions can be attached to the exposed graph/solver; the shared EV master does not add cut separation or tree search. The tiny regression comparisons establish agreement on the tested fixtures, with no performance or large-instance scaling claim.

Shared route master and column generation

EvMaster<M: LpMaster> uses Baldes's existing LP backend interface. Supply an empty backend, the EV problem, its initial departure time, and an optional nonnegative fleet limit. The master adds one set-partitioning equality per customer and an optional fleet upper-bound row. Only complete depot-to-depot customer-serving routes are columns. Every inserted route is independently replayed against the original problem, and its cost and customer coefficients are recomputed. The same stop order with different charging amounts is one travel-cost column.

No seed routes are required. Phase I minimizes artificial customer coverage, pricing with zero travel cost and the current coverage/fleet duals. When artificial usage reaches zero to tolerance, artificials are fixed to zero and the original travel costs are restored. Phase II prices against that objective. The two EV backends use exactly these same rows, objectives and tolerances.

Before pricing, the adapter checks the LP solution's primal row feasibility, dual signs, column reduced costs, objective and primal/dual agreement. A negative replay-validated column can improve the pool even after incomplete pricing. Convergence requires an optimal restricted LP and Complete pricing with no reduced cost below the requested tolerance. Bounded, work limits and numerical failures cannot establish full-model LP convergence or infeasibility. Positive phase-I usage establishes numerical infeasibility only after complete pricing and a positive adjusted dual objective; tolerance-sized negative reduced costs are accounted for by decreasing customer duals. This is still f64 validation, not an interval-arithmetic certificate.

Results distinguish convergence, phase-I infeasibility, incomplete pricing, LP stop, iteration/time limits and numerical failures. They retain phase and pricing statistics per pricing call, plus the last validated LP snapshot. A snapshot taken before the last column insertion is a feasible point with zero weight on later columns; it does not claim optimality of the updated pool. travel_objective is available only when artificial coverage is zero to tolerance. A restricted-pool objective is never labeled a full-model lower bound. optimize can be called again after a work/iteration/time limit, retaining the route pool and the current phase.

The adapter is generic over LpMaster; the integration tests below use Clp. For example, with an existing problem:

Rust
use baldes::extensions::evrp::{EvMaster, EvColumnGenerationOptions};
use baldes::master::clp::ClpMaster;
let mut master = EvMaster::new(problem, ClpMaster::new()?, 0.0, Some(3.0))?;
let result = master.optimize(EvColumnGenerationOptions::default())?;
// Inspect result.status before interpreting its last LP snapshot.

LP solves receive the remaining time budget. Pricing uses its work/label limits, with elapsed time checked before and after the call; the wall-time setting is not an interrupt inside an individual pricing operation.

Reproducible tiny comparisons

Run cargo run --features clp --example evrp_compare for a CSV comparison of all three backends with identical data and tolerances (1e-7 for LP and reduced cost). On the checked fixtures:

Case Direct LP Subpath LP Full route-pool LP Meaning
Three customers, route capacity two, fleet limit two 4.5 4.5 4.5 Fractional weights of 0.5 demonstrate LP rather than integer optimality.
Two customers, nonlinear charging required, fleet limit one 5.0 5.0 5.0 Returned routes require partial charging and pass replay.

The test reference pool enumerates every tiny stop order and constructs charging schedules independently on an integer-energy grid. For these integral fixtures, every order with no grid witness is also checked for continuous infeasibility by the fixed-order optimizer. The full pool LP is built separately from column generation, and the fractional case also has an analytic objective of 4.5. Additional tests cover fleet/reachability infeasibility, an empty customer set, seed validation and deduplication, phase-I initialization without seeds, resuming after limits, and bounded/work-limited pricing without convergence.

The CSV reports columns, pricing calls, labels, retained subpaths and algebra work. These tiny reference counts do not establish a runtime advantage or scalability on published instances.

Run cargo test --lib evrp:: for model/algebra/pricing tests and cargo test --features clp --lib evrp:: to include shared-master comparisons.

Supported scope and remaining extensions

The raw ElectricRouting.jl multi-depot data is downloaded under instances/evrp/electricrouting-jl/: 161 instances and 312 upstream reference solutions, pinned to commit 102c9c90f6021d2dea664f0d8ad6990661c7668a with per-file hashes. These files need a format reader and multi-depot integration before equivalent Baldes runs; they are not inputs to evrp_compare. Their nonlinear charging curves must be specified separately to match an upstream nonlinear experiment.

This implements the initial static, single-vehicle-type travel-cost EV LP workflow. It is not an integer branch-and-price solver. Cuts, branching/ng state, mixed fleets, tariffs, congestion, time-dependent travel, external benchmark parsers and large-instance performance engineering remain extensions. The E1 reference backend and E2-inspired prototype do not reproduce every acceleration from either paper. The native backend uses the explicit charging-front resource; scalar resource pricing alone does not establish nonlinear charging feasibility.
